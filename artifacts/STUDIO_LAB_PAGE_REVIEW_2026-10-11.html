Individual Studio lab pages: learning readiness and next improvements

11 October 2026 · 210 page entries · source review with earlier audit evidence as context. Studio home and curriculum pages are excluded. This is a preliminary source-based review, not a fresh browser test or a measured learning-outcomes study. Catalog entries, explicit lab routes and current investigation definitions are included; dynamic concept routes, redirects and gallery/advanced hubs are excluded. Some cross-studio links reuse an engine and should not be counted as distinct models.

Ranking: Rank 1 = strongest candidates for a focused learning pilot; Rank 2 = promising interactive foundation; Rank 3 = individual review required before ranking reliably. Pages within each rank are tied and displayed alphabetically. These are editorial readiness groups, not /100 measurements or evidence of world leadership. Rank 3 reflects limited review evidence, not a demonstrated product defect. The earlier inherited studio scores are not used.

Main finding: A responsive simulation becomes a complete learning lab when the learner can predict, experiment, explain, receive useful feedback on reasoning, and solve an unfamiliar problem. Existing labs already have many explanations, challenges, live models and shared state. New investigation pages already include prediction/reflection, limits, baseline comparison and numeric checks. Their shared answer feedback is generic and their export explicitly states that proof grading is absent.

Recommendations versus confirmed gaps: The “next improvement” column is a page-specific product target, not a claim that every named component is completely absent. Exact absence requires a deeper individual code and browser review. The shared investigation feedback limitation and inherited-score limitation were directly observed in source. Older reports contain stale gaps: modelling import/validation, graph matching/flow, and DE boundary/PDE tools already have implementations.

World-class acceptance target for every page: define one observable learning objective; connect actions to diagram and mathematics; provide progressively reduced hints; assess misconceptions and transfer; preserve evidence of reasoning; validate mathematical edge cases and approximation limits; verify touch, keyboard and accessible equivalents; measure improvement and delayed retention with learners. These are proposed product criteria, not a claim that all are missing everywhere.

Best learning sequence: a short prerequisite check → one prediction with a reason → a controlled experiment → compare representations → explain the surprise → solve a changed problem → revisit later. Prediction and explanation are consistent with PhET’s teaching guidance; classroom visibility and discussion have precedents in Desmos’s activity dashboard design. The particular sequence and ranking here are our editorial recommendations.

Build priority: first improve reasoning feedback and unseen transfer tasks in the rank-1 pilot pages; then reuse the proven learning loop across rank-2 pages. Review rank-3 pages individually before deciding whether they need rebuilding. Benchmark arbitrary-input handling and difficult cases for advanced labs rather than adding more topic cards.
