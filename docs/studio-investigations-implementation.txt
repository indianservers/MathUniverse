STUDIO INVESTIGATIONS — IMPLEMENTATION RECORD

Delivered: 57 interactive investigations, three in each of the 19 registered studios.
Entry point: choose Interactive Investigations in the studio navigation, or open a studio's Lessons & Practice checklist. Each investigation is also included in that studio's searchable reference library.
Routes: /studios/{studioId}/labs and /studios/{studioId}/labs/{investigationId}.

LEARNING FLOW
1. Predict an outcome and record the reason before revealing the computation.
2. Change sliders, numeric inputs, data tables, matrices, transitions or coefficients. Evidence updates live after reveal. Capture a baseline to compare metric changes, or reset the worked example.
3. Follow the input-specific derivation and inspect the misconception correction.
4. Solve a parameter-dependent transfer exercise. Blank, invalid and incorrect responses receive distinct feedback; numeric fractions are accepted. Worked answers are available for learning.
5. Explain the evidence and a boundary case. Download a JSON report containing inputs, conditions, computed results, derivation, prediction and reflection.
Written proofs and reflections are self-assessed. A numeric check does not establish a universal proof.

MATHEMATICAL BOUNDARIES
Every lab states its supported family and input constraints. These are teaching investigations, not claims of unrestricted symbolic solving or complete university-course equivalence.
Matrix teaching inputs are bounded to 8×8; data regressions accept 4–200 complete records and up to five predictors for multiple linear fitting. Field extensions are monic quadratics over p=2,3,5,7. PDE profiles use finite sine expansions on a unit interval/strip. BVP forcing supports constant or sine families with mixed left and Dirichlet right boundaries. Adaptive RK4 currently investigates scalar decay. Planarity may be inconclusive when an explicit obstruction is not found; passing an edge bound is never labelled proof of planarity.
Exact integer certificates, analytic identities and approximate numerical computations are distinguished. Singular/invalid/out-of-range inputs produce explanations. Thin SVD zero columns do not claim a full orthonormal null-space completion. Numerical overflow is rejected rather than presented as a meaningful result.
Topic-specific reading links point to university course/schedule material and OpenStax/NIST references. They support further learning, not an assertion that the university prescribes these exact interactive activities.
